Supernote Manta User Experience — Real-World Travel Review

Overview:
Sheila Mac's hands-on impressions of the Supernote Manta (A5x2) after traveling with it through Europe for three weeks. Based on a test prototype.

Key Advantages:

Portability and Comfort:
The device features a notably thinner profile compared to earlier models. The slimmer design enables single-handed landscape operation — particularly valuable for users with smaller hands. Much easier to hold for extended journaling or sketching sessions.

Display Quality:
The Manta's 300 DPI resolution (1920x2560) provides consistency with artwork across Supernote's product line, eliminating visual discrepancies experienced on older models.

Half-Folio Design:
The detachable folio is durable and practical. It survived coffee spills (washable) and doubles as a straight-edge tool for drawing — an unexpected but useful feature.

Durability:
The device survived a significant drop onto cobblestones while the reviewer was drawing atop York Wall, with minimal cosmetic damage. Robust enough for travel and outdoor use.

Notable Drawbacks:

Pen Loop:
The canvas-like pen loop material seemed fragile. The reviewer purchased adhesive alternatives as backup to prevent wear and damage to the loop.

Practical Advice for New Users:
The Manta represents a meaningful upgrade from the A5x for those prioritizing portability. However, existing A5x users satisfied with their current setup don't need to feel pressured to upgrade immediately — the core Supernote experience is the same.

For Bullet Journaling:
The thinner profile and single-handed landscape mode make the Manta more comfortable for extended BuJo sessions. The 300 DPI screen makes handwriting look crisper, which matters for layout-heavy spreads. The folio doubles as a ruler for drawing borders and headers.
