Mastering Digital Organization: A Minimalist Approach to Note Folders

The article addresses the challenge of digital note management, explaining how unstructured systems create chaos. The author proposes a minimalist solution for Supernote users.

Key Recommendations:

1. Streamline Categories
Rather than proliferating folders, consolidate related content. Combine "Work Projects," "Personal Projects," and "Hobbies" into a single "Projects" folder for better organization.

2. Limit Subfolders
"Resist the temptation to create endless layers of subfolders. Instead, aim for a shallow hierarchy with only a few levels of nesting." Two levels is usually enough on the Supernote.

3. Use Descriptive Naming
Apply clear, concise folder names that accurately describe their contents while avoiding vague terminology. Avoid generic names like "Notes" or "Misc."

4. Archive Regularly
Review and archive outdated notes periodically to maintain a lean, efficient system without digital clutter. The Supernote's Documents folder works well as an archive.

Benefits of the Minimalist Approach:
- Faster note retrieval — fewer places to look
- Reduced mental fatigue from navigating complex hierarchies
- Clearer organizational comprehension
- Greater system flexibility for future changes

This methodology helps users focus on productivity rather than struggling with complex folder hierarchies. For bullet journaling on Supernote, this means keeping a single BuJo notebook rather than splitting spreads across multiple folders.
